Accessibility

Last updated: 12 November 2026

We want everyone to be able to use Symly. We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. This statement covers four things: this website, the Symly practice app that dental teams use, the patient portal, and the reception tablet that patients use to fill in forms.

We have not met this aim in every place yet. Each section below says what we tested, and what we know does not work well.

This website

What you can do

  • zoom in up to 400% without text running off the screen;
  • use the whole website with a keyboard, starting from a link that skips to the content;
  • use a screen reader: pages have headings, landmarks and text alternatives for images;
  • use your device’s settings for text size.

How we tested it

On 2 November 2026 we checked every page with automated accessibility testing, at desktop and mobile sizes, against WCAG 2.2 level AA. We fixed the colour contrast problems it found.

Known limits

The pictures of the Symly app on our home page show made-up examples and use very small text, which can be hard to read. They hold no information you need: the text around them describes the same features. Screen readers announce a description of each picture, or read out its example text.

The Symly practice app (for dental teams)

What you can do

  • use the signed-in screens with a keyboard, starting from a link that skips to the content;
  • hear a clear page title and one main heading on each screen;
  • see where the keyboard focus is, and reach every control with a large enough target;
  • choose light or dark mode, one of five colour themes (one has very high contrast), and a larger text size;
  • use a screen reader to read each tooth on the dental chart, because each tooth has a text name that lists its findings.

How we tested it

On 12 November 2026 we ran automated accessibility testing on the signed-in screens against WCAG 2.2 level AA. We ran it in light and dark mode, and in every colour theme for the main screens. We also checked that screens fit a 320 pixel wide window, and that they fit at 200% zoom with the largest text size. We ran these tests in Chrome, and a few of them in a Safari-type browser engine (WebKit).

Known limits

  • You paint the surfaces of a tooth on the dental chart with a mouse or touch. There is no keyboard way to do this yet.
  • The findings on a tooth show in a pop-up only when you hover over the tooth. The text name for screen readers has the same facts.
  • The dental chart and the gum (periodontal) chart have no table version.
  • Some actions need dragging with no single-tap alternative yet: reordering treatment slots, assigning a pending payment allocation, and moving the dividers between panels. Sticky notes move with the arrow keys only.
  • The week and month diary, the day diary with several clinicians, both charts and wide tables scroll in two directions. They do not reflow to one column.
  • No person who uses a screen reader has tested the app, and we have not tested it in Firefox.

Time limits

A staff session ends 12 hours after sign-in. We are adding a lock screen after 30 minutes without use, with a warning first. We are also adding a warning 10 minutes before the 12 hours end. Until we ship these, the app does not warn you.

The patient portal

What you can do

  • use every page with a keyboard, starting from a link that skips to the content;
  • hear each new page announced, and each message that appears, with a screen reader;
  • fill in forms where each error is next to its field, says what is wrong, and moves your focus to the first one;
  • choose a colour theme, and zoom in or enlarge text without the page scrolling sideways;
  • find the link to this statement at the foot of every page.

How we tested it

On 12 November 2026 we ran automated accessibility testing on 36 pages and states of the portal, at phone and desktop size, against WCAG 2.2 level AA. We also ran scripted checks of the keyboard, focus, spoken messages, 200% text size and colour contrast in every theme. We ran these tests in Chrome.

Known limits

  • Two data tables (appointments and documents) scroll sideways on a small screen.
  • The practice writes its own forms, treatment plans and documents. We cannot check the practice’s wording or the files it uploads.
  • Stripe runs the card payment page. Its accessibility is Stripe’s.
  • Cancelling an appointment uses your browser’s own confirm box. Its look and wording come from your browser.
  • You draw a signature on a form with a finger or a mouse. We are adding a typed signature. Until then, a patient who cannot draw signs at the practice.
  • No person who uses a screen reader has tested the portal, and we have tested only in Chrome.

Time limits

  • The portal signs you out after 1 hour without use. It warns you 5 minutes before, and you can choose to stay signed in.
  • A session also ends 12 hours after sign-in, even if you stay active. The portal warns you 10 minutes before and again 2 minutes before.
  • The warning is a dialog. Focus moves into it, and you can answer with the keyboard.
  • Before the 12 hours end, the portal keeps what you typed on the page. It puts it back when you sign in again. It never keeps a password or a drawn signature.

The reception tablet

What you can do

  • fill in a form in portrait or landscape, in light or dark mode;
  • tap controls that are at least 44 pixels high and wide;
  • hear each new screen announced, because focus moves to its heading;
  • see a list of problems after a failed send, with a link to each one;
  • sign by drawing or by typing your name;
  • take as long as you need: the tablet has no time limit on a form;
  • open “Accessibility help” on any screen to see where to find this statement.

How we tested it

On 12 November 2026 we ran automated accessibility testing on 34 screens and states of the tablet against WCAG 2.2 level AA, in portrait and landscape, in light and dark mode. We also ran scripted checks of target size, keyboard order, focus, 200% text size and the typed signature. We ran these tests in Chrome.

Known limits

  • We have not tested VoiceOver on an iPad or TalkBack on an Android tablet.
  • We measured the text on the coloured panel by hand, because the automated tool cannot.
  • Your browser can fill in a field for you only for phone and email fields.
  • The practice decides whether a typed signature is valid for each form.
  • The practice writes the forms. The tablet cannot check the practice’s own wording.
  • If you need another way to answer, ask a member of staff.

What we have not done yet

Automated testing finds only some problems. We have not yet had Symly tested by people who use assistive technology. We also have not yet tested with real screen readers such as NVDA, VoiceOver or TalkBack, and we have not tested in Firefox. We do not claim any accessibility certification.

Tell us about a problem

If you find something hard to use, or need information in a different format, email hello@symlydental.com. We reply within 5 working days.
